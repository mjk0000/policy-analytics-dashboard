# Policy Comparison Feature Guide

## Overview

The **Policy Comparison** tab provides a comprehensive analysis comparing current economic outcomes with projected impacts of leading progressive policy proposals from major research organizations.

## Featured Organizations

This analysis incorporates research and policy proposals from:

1. **Economic Policy Institute (EPI)** - Labor economics and wage policy
2. **Center on Budget and Policy Priorities (CBPP)** - Federal budget and safety net programs
3. **Center for American Progress (CAP)** - Progressive policy across multiple domains
4. **The Roosevelt Institute** - Structural economic reform and power dynamics
5. **Demos** - Racial and economic justice policy
6. **Center for Law and Social Policy (CLASP)** - Low-income families and workforce development
7. **Brookings Institution** - Independent policy research
8. **Pew Research Center** - Data-driven social trends analysis

## Individual Policy Category Details

### Minimum Wage Proposals

#### $15 Federal Minimum Wage (EPI)
- **Current**: $7.25/hour ($15,080/year)
- **Proposed**: $15.00/hour ($31,200/year)
- **Impact**: +$1,342/month for minimum wage workers
- **Affected workers**: 27 million directly
- **Timeline**: Phase in over 5 years, then index to median wage growth

#### Living Wage Standard (EPI)
- **Current**: $7.25/hour
- **Proposed**: $18.00/hour (~$37,440/year)
- **Impact**: +$1,864/month for minimum wage workers
- **Affected workers**: 35 million
- **Rationale**: Ensures all workers can afford basic necessities based on Family Budget Calculator

### Tax Policy Proposals

#### Expand EITC (CBPP)
- **For workers under $30k**: +$125/month
- **For workers $30k-$50k**: +$67/month
- **Phases out above $50k**
- Particularly helps workers without children who receive minimal EITC currently

#### Wealth Tax on Ultra-Rich (CAP)
- **2% annual tax on net worth above $50M**
- **3% annual tax on net worth above $1B**
- **No impact on median household** (net worth $192,900)
- **Estimated revenue**: $200B annually to fund social programs

#### Progressive Income Tax Reform (Roosevelt Institute)
- **70% top marginal rate on income over $10M**
- **Close capital gains loopholes**
- **No tax increase for households under $400k**
- **Revenue**: $750B over 10 years

### Housing Proposals

#### Universal Housing Vouchers (CBPP)
- **Eligibility**: Income below 50% of area median
- **Benefit**: Reduces rent burden to 30% of income
- **Eligible households**: 10.7 million
- **Impact**: Varies by household - can be $300-$800/month for eligible low-income renters

#### Public Housing Investment (CAP)
- **Annual investment**: $70 billion
- **Units created**: 500,000 per year (5 million over 10 years)
- **Indirect benefit**: Market rent reduction of ~$150/month over 5 years from increased supply

#### National Rent Stabilization (Demos)
- **Cap annual rent increases at 3% or inflation, whichever is lower**
- **Current rent inflation**: ~5-6% annually
- **Savings**: ~$25/month on $1,650 rent, compounds over time
- **Affected**: All 44 million U.S. renters

### Healthcare Proposals

#### Medicare for All (CAP & Multiple)
- **Eliminates**: Premiums, deductibles, copays
- **Funding**: 4% income tax
- **Median household**: Saves $132/month
- **Median individual**: Saves $153/month
- **Most significant savings for lower-income households currently paying high premiums**

#### Expand ACA (CBPP)
- **Premium cap**: 8.5% of income
- **Add public option**
- **Increase subsidies**
- **Impact**: Mainly helps households currently paying >8.5% of income on healthcare

### Childcare & Family Proposals

#### Universal Child Care (CLASP)
- **Free universal pre-K**
- **Childcare capped at 7% of income**
- **Current average**: $1,200/month for infant care
- **Savings for median household with infant**: ~$700/month

#### Permanent Child Tax Credit (CAP)
- **$3,600/year per child under 6** ($300/month)
- **$3,000/year per child 6-17** ($250/month)
- **Fully refundable** - benefits low-income families
- **Monthly payment option**
- **Impact on child poverty**: 40% reduction (CBPP analysis)

### Education & Student Debt

#### Debt-Free College (Demos)
- **Free tuition at public colleges and universities**
- **Expanded Pell Grants for living expenses**
- **Prevents future debt** - saves $10k-$20k/year in tuition
- **Does not cancel existing $1.7T in student debt**

#### Student Debt Cancellation (Roosevelt Institute)
- **Cancel $50,000 per borrower**
- **Affects 43 million borrowers**
- **For typical borrower with $30k debt**: Eliminates ~$300/month payment
- **Immediate disposable income increase for affected households**

### Transportation

#### Expanded Public Transit (Brookings)
- **Annual investment**: $100 billion in infrastructure and operations
- **Potential savings**: $920/month for those who can switch from car to transit
- **Car ownership**: $1,015/month
- **Public transit**: $95/month
- **Estimate**: 30% of car owners gain viable transit option

## Important Methodological Notes

### Simplified Projections

These are **illustrative projections** for policy analysis purposes. Actual impacts would vary based on:

1. **Household specifics**:
   - Income level (policies have different impacts at different income levels)
   - Household composition (children, adults, elderly)
   - Current employment status
   - Location (urban, suburban, rural)
   - Current benefits received

2. **Policy implementation details**:
   - Phase-in schedules
   - Regional variations
   - Eligibility criteria
   - Interaction effects between policies

3. **Economic dynamics**:
   - Behavioral responses
   - Market adjustments
   - Macroeconomic effects
   - Employer responses (for wage policies)

### Data Sources

- **Baseline data**: Census Bureau median income, BLS Consumer Expenditure Survey
- **Policy proposals**: Published research from listed organizations
- **Impact estimates**: Based on organization research, peer-reviewed studies, and CBO scoring where available

### Limitations

1. **Static analysis**: Does not account for dynamic economic responses
2. **Median focus**: Impacts vary significantly across income distribution
3. **Aggregate level**: Individual circumstances differ substantially
4. **Simplified calculations**: Real policy implementation is more complex
5. **Point-in-time**: Policy proposals evolve and details change

### Best Practices for Using This Data

✅ **DO**:
- Use as illustrative examples of policy direction and magnitude
- Compare relative impacts across different policy approaches
- Understand which policies target which problems
- Explore trade-offs and priorities
- Follow links to original research for full details

❌ **DON'T**:
- Treat projections as precise predictions
- Assume your specific household matches median exactly
- Ignore implementation challenges and political feasibility
- Conflate proposals from different organizations without noting differences
- Overlook that these are progressive organization proposals, not neutral analyses
